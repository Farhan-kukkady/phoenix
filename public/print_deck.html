The Real-World Problem

What Happens When a Customer Visits the Website Today?

Why 95% of interested factory managers visit the site and leave without buying anything.

1

The "200-Door Maze" (Too Many Products)

Imagine walking into a hardware store with 200 different unlabeled tools and no staff to help you. A packaging manager comes to the website wanting to check potato chip bags or perfume cartons. Instead of simple guidance, they see 21 different brand logos and 200+ machines. They get overwhelmed in 30 seconds, close the browser, and leave.

2

The "Physics Degree Required" (Technical Jargon)

The website was talking like a science textbook: "45°:0° optical geometry, M3 polarization, d/8 integrating spheres." A real factory manager doesn't care about physics terms—they just want to know: "Will this machine work on my shiny candy foil without making mistakes?" Because they don't understand the jargon, they freeze and refuse to commit.

3

The "$20,000 Sticker Shock" (Price Hesitation)

These machines cost between $15,000 and $25,000+. When a customer sees that price (or a blank "Request Quote" button) with no explanation of how much money the machine will save them, their CFO immediately kills the project. They see it as an expensive cost instead of a money-saving investment.

4

The "Ghost Town After 6 PM" (Lost Night Shifts)

Printing factories in Saudi Arabia, UAE, and Africa run 24 hours a day, including night shifts. When a plant manager looks for a solution at 9:00 PM and only sees a contact form saying "We are open Mon-Fri 8am-6pm", they won't wait 12 hours. They click the next Google link and contact a competitor.

The Core Lesson: If our website doesn't answer "Which machine fits me?" and "How much money will I save?", customers will never buy.
